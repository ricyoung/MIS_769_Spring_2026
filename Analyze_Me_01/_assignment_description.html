Your Report Should Tell a Story

The most common mistakes on this assignment are: (1) jumping straight into code without explaining why you're looking at this data, (2) not telling me what you did to clean it, and (3) not wrapping up with a conclusion. Your report needs a beginning, a middle, and an end.

Here's the flow:

  1. The Problem: What question are you trying to answer? Why does this topic matter? (Even one sentence is fine, just give me the "why.")
  2. The Data: What dataset did you find? Where did it come from? How big is it?
  3. The Cleaning: What did you have to fix, remove, or transform, and why? Don't just say "I removed stopwords." Tell me why you removed them and what difference it made.
  4. The Analysis: What techniques did you apply? What do your visualizations show?
  5. The Conclusion: What did you find? Does it answer your original question? What surprised you? What would you do next?

Example

Problem: "Are customers complaining about the same things, or is every complaint unique?"

Data: "I downloaded 50,000 Amazon product reviews from Kaggle."

Cleaning: "I removed 2,300 duplicate reviews and filtered out entries with no review text. I also removed stopwords because words like 'the' and 'and' were drowning out the actual complaint terms."

Analysis: "I ran sentiment analysis, built a word cloud of 1-star reviews, and used NER to extract product names."

Conclusion: "It turns out 60% of negative reviews mention 'shipping' or 'packaging'. The products themselves aren't the problem. A company looking at this data should fix their logistics, not their product line."

Tips

  • Use what we've learned: pandas, matplotlib, seaborn for any data. For text data: spaCy, NLTK, HuggingFace, word clouds, sentiment, NER.
  • Start simple. Load the data, look at it, clean it. Don't jump to fancy models before understanding your data.
  • One good question beats ten shallow ones. Pick something specific and dig deep.
  • Visualize. Bar charts, scatter plots, heatmaps, distributions, word clouds, maps. A good chart is worth a thousand words.
  • Google and AI are your friends. Use ChatGPT/Claude to help with code or interpretation. Use Google to sanity-check your findings.
  • Have fun with it. The best projects come from genuine curiosity. Pick data you actually want to explore.
